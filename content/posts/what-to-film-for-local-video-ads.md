Your next content day does not need a complicated set. For a local service business, the most useful material is often already there: the people doing the work, the steps customers rarely see, and the answers you give every week.

A clear shot list helps turn that everyday knowledge into advertising someone can understand.

## Start with one customer and one job

Choose the service you want the video to support before deciding what to film. “Everything we do” is a difficult message to communicate in a short ad. A specific job gives the viewer something concrete to recognize.

For example, a screening company could explain what a homeowner should consider before enclosing a patio. A pool business could show the steps between an initial conversation and a finished project. The setting and footage should support that particular story.

Write down the person you want to reach, the problem they have, and the next action you want them to take. Keep those three points close throughout the shoot.

## Build a simple shot list

- **The people:** introduce the owner or team member a customer may actually speak with. Use their own words and a comfortable pace.
- **The setting:** capture the location, work area and details that make the business recognizable.
- **The process:** show a few meaningful steps. Explain what the team is checking and why it matters.
- **The finished work:** include enough context for a viewer to understand what changed. Use accurate before-and-after footage from the same job when available.
- **Common questions:** record short answers about preparation, timing, the service area and what happens after an inquiry.
- **The invitation:** explain the next step, whether that is requesting an estimate, calling the team or booking a consultation.

Ask for permission before filming customers, private property or anything identifying. Check the background for paperwork, license plates or personal information that should stay out of the edit.

## Capture useful variations

Record more than one opening. One can lead with the customer's problem; another can start with the result they are looking for. Keep the rest of the message focused so you can compare the openings without changing everything at once.

Get a clean take without music or graphics added on location. Clear audio, steady framing and room around the subject make the footage easier to adapt to different placements later.

## Let the real business come through

Preparation should make the team more comfortable, not force everyone into the same script. A useful explanation in the owner's natural voice can give a prospect a better sense of the people they would hire.
