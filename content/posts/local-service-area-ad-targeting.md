Your service area is a business decision before it is an advertising setting. A nearby inquiry can still be a poor fit if it asks for the wrong work, creates an impractical drive or falls outside the jobs your crew can take on.

Our Instagram targeting lesson argues against choosing a few ZIP codes and hoping the campaign finds suitable customers. The useful starting point is your own job history: where good projects come from, what customers need and which areas you can serve well.

## Map the work you actually want

Review recent completed jobs and suitable estimates. For each one, record the area, service requested, travel burden and whether the project matched your preferred work. You do not need to expose customer identities in a campaign-planning document.

Separate three groups:

- **Core areas:** Work you can serve consistently and profitably.
- **Conditional areas:** Places you serve for certain projects, schedules or minimum job sizes.
- **Outside scope:** Locations your team should not be promising to cover.

Ask operations to review the map. The person scheduling the crew may see problems that are invisible in an advertising report.

## Explain the service as clearly as the location

A geographic boundary does not tell someone whether you do repairs, replacements or new installations. Make the service clear in the ad and on the destination page. Include any meaningful limitation before someone spends time requesting the wrong thing.

For example, a business might serve a wider area for larger installations than for small repairs. That is an illustrative business rule, not a recommendation to exclude any particular community. Use your actual operational requirements and apply them consistently.

## Do not confuse an audience theory with proven demand

The reel mentions data such as home value and behavior. Treat those as ideas to evaluate, not proof that a person needs a service or a guarantee that a particular advertising control exists today. Platform options and campaign restrictions change.

Customer-stated needs and actual sales outcomes are more useful feedback than assumptions alone. A lead form or first conversation can establish the project type and service area directly.

## Compare areas by suitable opportunities

Use a small reporting table with a consistent time window:

| Area | Inquiries | Suitable inquiries | Appointments held | Jobs won | Common mismatch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core area A | Track | Track | Track | Track | Record the reason |
| Core area B | Track | Track | Track | Track | Record the reason |

An area with cheap inquiries can become expensive if most cannot be served. An area with fewer inquiries may still produce useful work. Compare completed sales periods so a long estimate cycle does not make a newer group look worse prematurely.

## Change one clear mismatch first

If people repeatedly ask for work outside your scope, clarify the message. If the location is wrong, review coverage and how location information is collected. If suitable prospects never receive a response, fix that handoff before blaming the area.

Start with a map your crew can honor, an offer customers understand and a consistent record of what happens after the inquiry. That gives targeting decisions a practical foundation.
